02 AROMATICS · ORIGIN OF THE NAMECH.02

Naphtha from the refining process passes through petrochemical processes and is converted into aromatics such as benzene, toluene, mixed xylene and para-xylene.

芳 香
Aromatics · The fragrant family

‘Aromatic’ means fragrant. When 19th-century chemists first classified this family, many of the compounds they found really did have pleasant scents — and so they became ‘the fragrant family’. The East Asian name, 芳香, likewise means fragrance.

Today the name has nothing to do with scent. It now refers to ring compounds like benzene whose electrons spread evenly around the ring, making them unusually stable. A name that began with fragrance has come to describe a structure.

03 OLEFINS · ORIGIN OF THE NAMECH.03

Hydrocarbons such as naphtha and LPG are thermally cracked at high temperature into smaller molecules, then quenched, compressed and separated to produce basic petrochemicals such as ethylene and propylene.

OLÉFIANT
Olefins · The oil-making gas

In the late 18th century, chemists found that ethylene combined with chlorine produced oily liquid droplets. They named it the ‘oil-making gas’ (gaz oléfiant) — and that is where the word olefin comes from.

Ethylene and propylene are typical olefins with a carbon-carbon double bond (C=C). Highly reactive, they are basic raw materials for a wide range of petrochemical products, including polyethylene (PE) and polypropylene (PP).

DATA — gscaltex.com/kr/business/mfc16
MECHANISM · THERMAL CRACKINGCH.03

Breaking down hydrocarbons with intense heat

01 — MIXED FEED

Using liquid and gas feedstocks together

A typical naphtha cracker (NCC) handles only naphtha, but the MFC also feeds gaseous feedstocks such as ethane and LPG into its furnaces, and takes refinery off-gas into the back end. Low-value refinery by-products become feedstock.

That's the Mixed in the name
02 — CRACKING

Cracking with intense heat

The high temperature of the cracking furnace breaks the long hydrocarbon chains. The output is then quenched, compressed and separated into individual products.

Thermal cracking, not boiling-point separation
03 — OLEFINS · DOUBLE BOND

Basic petrochemicals such as ethylene and propylene

The carbon-carbon double bond (C=C) is highly reactive and readily bonds with other molecules. Ethylene and propylene, which have this property, are produced as basic petrochemicals.

04 POLYMER · ORIGIN OF THE NAMECH.04

Basic petrochemicals such as ethylene and propylene go through polymerization to become polyethylene (PE), polypropylene (PP) and other polymer products.

POLY · MEROS
Polymer · Many parts

From the Greek poly (many) and meros (part) — a polymer is literally ‘something made of many parts’. In East Asian languages it is 重合體, ‘a substance joined over and over’.

A macromolecule formed when small molecules called monomers bond repeatedly; through polymerization it becomes the material for films, containers, pipes, auto parts and more.

Produced in easy-to-process pellet form

01 — MONOMER

The basic raw material for polymers

Ethylene and propylene are the representative monomers used to produce polyethylene (PE) and polypropylene (PP).

Monomer (monomer)
02 — POLYMERIZATION

Joining monomers into macromolecules

Reacting monomers with catalysts such as Ziegler-Natta or metallocene to convert them into long-chain macromolecules is called polymerization.

Properties tuned by catalyst and process conditions
03 — PELLET

Easy-to-process pellet form

A polymer is a mass of countless tangled long chains. It is extruded, cooled and cut into rice-grain-sized pellets, which then go through various processing steps to be used in films, containers, pipes, auto parts and more.

05 BASE OIL · WHAT THE NAME MEANSCH.05

Using unconverted oil (UCO) from the hydrocracker (HCR) in the refinery's upgrading facilities, further refining turns it into high-quality base oil.

BASE OIL
Base Oil · The base of lubricants

Just as the name says, it's the oil that forms the base. Finished lubricants such as engine oil are over 90% base oil, with small amounts of additives fine-tuning performance. Because it makes up most of the finished product, base oil quality sets the foundation of lubricant performance.

Base oils have international grades (API Groups): Gr.II, with fewer impurities and more stable viscosity than the older Gr.I, and premium Gr.III. GS Caltex produces these high-quality grades with state-of-the-art hydrocracking technology and supplies them worldwide as Kixx LUBO.

DATA — gscaltex.com/kr/Business/BaseOil24
MECHANISM · BASE OIL PRODUCTIONCH.05

Advanced processes such as iso-dewaxing raise base oil performance

01 — FEEDSTOCK

Unconverted oil left by the upgrading unit

The oil the refinery's hydrocracker (HCR) couldn't fully break down — unconverted oil. This heavy feedstock, with its tangled molecules, is the starting point for base oil.

A low-value feed becomes the starting point
02 — ISO-DEWAXING

Iso-dewaxing raises performance

Processes including two-stage hydrocracking, iso-dewaxing and hydrofinishing remove impurities and improve viscosity characteristics and low-temperature fluidity so that viscosity performance holds up across temperature changes.

Better low-temperature fluidity and viscosity
03 — PREMIUM

Evenly aligned molecules make a clear premium

Gr.II and Gr.III produced flexibly from the same feed. Gr.III in particular, with its high viscosity index, is the heart of premium automotive and industrial lubricants.

Tighter emission rules = rising demand

LUBRICUS
Lubricants · Slippery

A lubricant forms a thin oil film between two surfaces moving against each other, reducing friction, wear and heat. It is used wherever there is motion, from car engines to factory equipment.

Lubricants are made by blending base oil with additives suited to the application and required performance, and base oil makes up over 90% of the finished product. GS Caltex makes Kixx products with in-house high-quality base oil and accumulated blending technology.

MECHANISM · HOW AN OIL FILM WORKSCH.06

When machines move, friction occurs between surfaces

01 — FRICTION

Rough surfaces touch directly

However smooth it looks, a metal surface is microscopically uneven. Move it as is and it scratches, heats up and wears away — friction and wear.

The challenge of every machine
02 — OIL FILM

A thin film seeps in between

When a lubricant forms an oil film between two surfaces, metal no longer meets metal directly. Surfaces that once scraped each other now glide on oil.

This film decides an engine's lifespan
03 — SMOOTH

Less friction, motion comes alive

So cars run with more power, ships glide on, and factory machines keep turning. 300+ Kixx products — in-house base oil plus the science of additives — each take charge of their own kind of motion.
