LUBRI-CUS LUBRICUS — LATIN FOR "SLIPPERY"

ORIGIN

Rooted in lubricus,
Latin for "slippery"

The word lubricant comes from the Latin lubricus, meaning "slippery."

A lubricant forms an oil film between two moving surfaces in contact, reducing friction and wear so that machines and equipment run smoothly.

It is widely used in applications ranging from car engines and transmissions to machinery across industrial sites.

01 — FRICTION

Moving machines create
friction between surfaces

When metal surfaces move in direct contact, friction and heat build up, and components can wear down.

01 — OIL FILM

Lubricant forms an oil film between the surfaces

Lubricant forms an oil film between moving surfaces, reducing direct contact to minimize friction and prevent wear.

01 — PERFORMANCE

Reducing friction and wear sustains performance

Using the right lubricant for the application protects components and helps machinery maintain stable performance.

FROM ENERGY TO MATERIALS · GS CALTEX BUSINESS

From crude to energy and materials,
connected in one value chain

GS Caltex's business starts with crude sourcing and refining and extends into a range of petrochemical products, including aromatics, olefins, and polymers. Feedstock from the refinery's upgrading process is turned into high-quality base oils, which in turn serve as the main raw material for Kixx lubricants. Close integration across its businesses and processes allows GS Caltex to make fuller use of feedstock and supply a wide range of energy and materials products to customers in Korea and overseas.

GS Caltex's business began with crude oil and has expanded beyond refining into petrochemicals, base oils, and lubricants. These businesses are closely connected through feedstock, processes, and technology, and together they produce energy and a wide range of industrial materials. From energy to materials, GS Caltex is building competitiveness for a better future.
